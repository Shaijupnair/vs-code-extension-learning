This is a **90-Day "Onboarding & Impact" Plan** tailored specifically for a Director of Engineering entering a global setup in India.

The goal of this plan is to transition you from "The New Hire" to " The Trusted Leader" by balancing local team health with global stakeholder confidence.

---

### Phase 1: Days 1–30 — The "Discovery" Phase

**Theme:** Listen, Learn, and Build Relationships. Do not make drastic changes yet.

**1. People & Culture (The Local Pulse)**

* **1:1s with Direct Reports (EMs):** Don't just ask "What do you do?" Ask "What keeps you up at night?" and "What is blocking your team?"
* **Skip-Level Meetings:** Meet with Staff/Principal Engineers and random key ICs to get the unfiltered ground truth.
* **Assess Retention Risk:** Review attrition data for the last 12 months. Identify "flight risks" (key people who seem disengaged) immediately.

**2. Stakeholder Alignment (The Global Bridge)**

* **Sync with Global Counterparts (US/EU):** Meet Product Directors and other Engineering Directors.
* **Understand the "India Perception":** Gently probe to understand how the India center is viewed by HQ. Is it seen as a partner, or just an execution arm? This defines your battle for the year.

**3. Technology & Product**

* **The "Shadow" Process:** Sit in on sprint demos, incident reviews, and architectural discussions without speaking much. Observe the dynamics.
* **Audit the Org Chart:** Does the structure match the delivery streams? Are teams too big (12+) or too small (<3)?

> **Deliverable at Day 30:** A **"State of the Union" Report**. A 2-page document outlining your observations on People, Process, and Tech, shared with your boss (VP/CTO).

---

### Phase 2: Days 31–60 — The "Assessment & Quick Wins" Phase

**Theme:** Formulate your thesis and fix the obvious broken windows.

**1. Operational Efficiency**

* **Identify "Quick Wins":** Find one low-hanging fruit that frustrates everyone (e.g., "Our CI/CD takes too long" or "Meetings with the US are too late"). **Fix it.** This buys you immense political capital.
* **Review Hiring Bar:** Sit in on interview debriefs. Ensure the India team isn't lowering the bar just to fill seats. Calibrate the process.

**2. Strategic Alignment**

* **Review OKRs/Roadmap:** Look at the current quarter's goals. Are we on track? If not, communicate the risk *now*, not at the end of the quarter.
* **Clarify Ownership:** Identify "Grey Zones"—areas where ownership between India and Global is unclear. Propose a clear split (e.g., "India owns the Payments Microservice end-to-end").

**3. Talent Assessment**

* **The EM Calibration:** Assess your managers. Who is a "scaler" (can handle more scope) and who is struggling?
* **Succession Map:** Identify who could be your "right hand."

> **Deliverable at Day 60:** A **Draft Strategic Roadmap**. A proposal of how you intend to restructure teams, change processes, or alter the tech roadmap for the next 6-12 months.

---

### Phase 3: Days 61–90 — The "Execution & Vision" Phase

**Theme:** Take the wheel. Start driving the changes you planned.

**1. Organizational Design**

* **Execute Structural Changes:** If you need to move people around or change reporting lines, do it now.
* **Establish "The Working Agreement":** Formalize how India collaborates with HQ (e.g., "No meetings after 9:30 PM IST," or "Async-first documentation").

**2. Long-Term Strategy**

* **Budget & Headcount:** Present your headcount request for the next year based on the business value you intend to deliver.
* **Tech Vision:** Champion a technical initiative (e.g., "We will reduce tech debt by 20% by adopting X").

**3. Establishing Culture**

* **Town Hall:** Host your first "All Hands." Articulate your vision for the India center. Move the narrative from "Task Completion" to "Product Ownership."

> **Deliverable at Day 90:** A **fully operational roadmap** and a team that understands their new direction. You are no longer "new"; you are the owner.

### Critical "Do's and Don'ts" for an India Director

| **DO** | **DON'T** |
| --- | --- |
| **Do** over-communicate with HQ. Silence is interpreted as failure in remote setups. | **Don't** become a "proxy." If your EMs can talk to US PMs directly, let them. Don't bottleneck information. |
| **Do** protect your team's work-life balance (WLB). Push back on 11 PM meetings. | **Don't** try to code. Your value is in unblocking, not merging PRs. |
| **Do** focus heavily on "Employer Brand" in India (LinkedIn presence, tech talks). | **Don't** ignore the "Quiet Quieters." In India, people often leave without complaining first. |

### Template for your "Listening Tour" (Days 1-15)

When you meet your new team members, ask these three specific questions to get high-value data:

1. *"If you were in my shoes, what is the **one thing** you would change immediately?"*
2. *"What is the most painful part of your day-to-day work?"* (Is it tools? Meetings? Legacy code?)
3. *"Who is the unsung hero of this organization that I should meet?"* (Helps you find hidden talent).
